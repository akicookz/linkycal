### 2. Fillout — best flexible all-rounder with a strong free plan

Fillout is a modern visual builder for multi-page forms, quizzes, and workflows. You can share a hosted link or embed. Creation options include drag-and-drop, prompts, imports, and PDF-to-form style starts. Question pacing is flexible: a few per page, all on one page, or one at a time.

**Verified free tier.** Unlimited forms, unlimited seats, and 1,000 responses per month. Payments collection (including Stripe) is available on the free plan. Most of the 50+ integrations (Airtable, Google Sheets, Notion, webhooks, HubSpot, Mailchimp, and more) are included on free as well.

**Paid plans.** Starter / Pro / Business tiers raise response caps (2,000 → 5,000 → unlimited on the public comparison table). Exact dollar prices on Fillout’s pricing UI can vary by billing cadence; check current pricing before you commit. CAPTCHA, heavier branding control, custom CSS, and analytics unlock on higher tiers.

**Strengths we noticed.** Seat-free collaboration is rare and valuable. Logic, calculations, scheduling fields, and PDF generation cover a lot of ops use cases without jumping to Jotform’s denser UI. Embedding and pretty share URLs are first-class. The free plan is credible for real campaigns, not just demos—as long as 1,000 responses / month fits.

**Watch-outs.** CAPTCHA and some branding controls sit above free. Analytics depth and custom CSS unlock later. If your primary need is a durable contact database with enrichment and follow-up workflows inside the same product, compare LinkyCal’s contacts layer rather than assuming Fillout’s integrations replace that work.

**Best for.** Product and ops teams that want a capable builder without paying per seat, and that may later build internal apps on top of form data.

**Not ideal for.** Teams that need a CRM-shaped contact database inside the form product itself. Fillout integrates well; it does not try to replace your CRM the way HubSpot or LinkyCal’s contacts layer do.

### 3. Tally — best unlimited free form builder

Tally’s pitch is blunt: unlimited forms and unlimited submissions on the free plan within fair-use guidelines. Conditional logic, payments, signatures, file uploads, password protection, and core integrations (Google Sheets, Notion, Airtable, Zapier, Make, webhooks) are available without a submission paywall.

**Paid plans.** Pro is listed at €20 per month (monthly billing; yearly saves two months). That removes branding, adds custom domains, collaboration workspaces, partial submissions, custom CSS, tailored email notifications, and deeper analytics. Business is listed at €65 per month for retention controls, email verification, and longer version history.

**Strengths we noticed.** The editor feels like writing a document. Conditional logic and payments without a response paywall remove the most common reason people abandon Typeform trials. EU-based GDPR posture and European data storage are a plus for many teams. Integrations cover the modern no-code stack without forcing you into a single CRM.

**Watch-outs.** “Unlimited” still sits under fair-use guidelines—read them if you run extremely high traffic. Advanced branding, custom domains, partial submissions, and richer email notification control are Pro features. There is no opinionated lite CRM inside Tally; your system of record is wherever you send the webhook or Sheet.

**Best for.** Indie makers, creators, and startups that would otherwise hit Typeform or Jotform response caps immediately. Anyone who wants a clean Notion-like writing experience for forms.

**Not ideal for.** Teams that want a built-in lite CRM and enrichment without wiring Notion or a third-party CRM. Also weaker if you need enterprise compliance packaging as a first-class product story.

**LinkyCal note.** If Tally’s free unlimited model is the only requirement, start there. If you keep exporting rows into a spreadsheet and rebuilding follow-ups by hand, compare [LinkyCal vs Tally](/alternatives/tally).

### 4. Typeform — best conversational experience

Typeform popularized the one-question-at-a-time form. It still leads when brand feel and completion experience matter more than raw throughput. Hosted forms, embeds, logic, and a large integration surface are all there.

**Pricing (verified from Typeform’s pricing page).** Basic is $39 / month billed monthly, or $28 / month billed yearly, with 100 responses / month and 1 user. Plus is $79 / month monthly ($56 yearly) with 1,000 responses and 3 users. Business is $129 / month monthly ($91 yearly) with 10,000 responses and 5 users. A free plan exists with a limited monthly response allowance; Typeform’s public FAQ does not hard-code the free response number on the pricing page we fetched, so check the live free plan before you plan a campaign around it.

**Strengths we noticed.** Motion, typography, and one-at-a-time pacing still produce higher perceived quality than most grid-style builders. Logic, endings, and integrations are mature. For brand campaigns and research, that polish is the product.

**Watch-outs.** Response math gets expensive when a form goes semi-viral. Basic’s 100 responses / month is a planning constraint, not a footnote. Contacts & Automations appear as add-ons on higher plans. If you mainly need a utilitarian intake form, you will overpay for aesthetics.

**Best for.** Marketing teams, research, and brand-sensitive lead forms where the form *is* the experience.

**Not ideal for.** High-volume capture on a budget. Free and entry paid caps are tight compared with Fillout, Tally, or forms.app. Contacts and automations are increasingly available as add-ons / higher plans rather than the default free path.

Jotform is the Swiss Army knife: huge template library, payment forms, PDFs, approvals, AI agents, and almost every specialty form type you can name. Share a link, embed, or publish as a fillable PDF-adjacent workflow.

**Best for.** Operations teams that need many form shapes (applications, waivers, order forms, appointment-adjacent intake) and prefer one vendor with templates for everything.

**Not ideal for.** Teams that hate per-form and per-submission ceilings on free, or that want a minimal UI. Jotform’s surface area is large; that is a feature and a learning cost.

### 6. Google Forms — best free default for Workspace teams

Google Forms remains the default for internal surveys, RSVPs, and lightweight data collection. Create a form, share the link, watch responses land in Sheets.

**Pricing.** Free with a Google account. Google does not market a submission paywall the way SaaS form vendors do. Design control and branding are limited. Conditional logic exists but is thinner than Fillout, Typeform, or LinkyCal. Spam and professional notification workflows are basic.

**Strengths.** Zero learning curve for Workspace users. Collaboration and Sheets pivot tables are immediate. Sharing permissions inherit Google’s familiar model.

**Watch-outs.** Branding is basically “this is a Google Form.” Public spam and lead-routing workflows are thin. There is no serious multi-product workflow layer for sales follow-up.

**Best for.** Schools, internal teams, and anyone already living in Google Sheets.

**Not ideal for.** Customer-facing brand forms, complex multi-step intake, or CRM-style contact management. If the form is part of your go-to-market motion, graduate to a dedicated builder.

### 7. HubSpot Forms — best when the CRM is already HubSpot

HubSpot’s form builder is free to start and exists to put people into HubSpot’s CRM. Drag-and-drop fields, templates, embed on your site or use standalone pages, automatic contact creation, follow-up email options, and spam filtering. Premium Marketing Hub features add conditional logic depth, form shortening, multi-step patterns, and heavier personalization.

**Pricing.** Forms are available on HubSpot’s free tier. Advanced automation and scale sit inside paid Marketing Hub editions (Starter / Professional / Enterprise). Check HubSpot’s current Marketing Hub pricing for package costs; forms are rarely sold as a standalone SKU.

**Strengths.** The form is not an island—submissions enrich the same contact timeline sales already uses. Free entry makes experimentation cheap if you are willing to live in HubSpot.

**Watch-outs.** Advanced conversion features sit behind Marketing Hub upgrades. If you only wanted a form and accidentally adopted a CRM migration, that is a strategic cost, not a line item on the forms page.

**Best for.** Teams standardized on HubSpot who want lead capture without a second database.

**Not ideal for.** Teams that want a beautiful standalone form brand outside the HubSpot ecosystem, or that refuse to keep contacts in HubSpot. Also awkward if your site stack fights HubSpot’s embed model.

### 8. WPForms — best form builder plugin for WordPress

WPForms is a WordPress plugin, not a hosted SaaS form first. You build in `wp-admin`, place forms with shortcodes or blocks, and store entries on your site (with optional cloud / integration routing). Conversational forms, landing pages, payments, and a large addon ecosystem exist on paid tiers. Lite (free) covers basic contact forms.

**Pricing (verified from WPForms pricing).** Basic list price is shown around $99 / year (introductory discounts appear on the page and renew at full price). Plus, Pro, and Elite raise site limits and unlock marketing, payment, and CRM integrations. Paid plans advertise unlimited forms and submissions on the licensed sites.

**Strengths.** Editors stay in WordPress. Entry management, notifications, and spam tools feel native. The addon ecosystem covers payments, surveys, user registration, and abandonment.

**Watch-outs.** You inherit WordPress hosting, plugin updates, and mail deliverability issues. Introductory pricing on the public page renews at full list—budget the renewal, not only the first-year discount. Lite is fine for basic contact forms; most serious sites end up on paid licenses.

**Best for.** WordPress agencies and site owners who want forms to live next to posts and pages with no third-party hosted URL as the primary surface.

**Not ideal for.** Non-WordPress stacks. If your marketing site is Webflow, Framer, or a custom app, a hosted SaaS link (Fillout, Tally, LinkyCal, Typeform) is usually simpler than forcing WordPress into the mix just for forms.

## How to choose among these 11

Start with constraints, not brand names.

1. **Where does the form live?** Need a link you can drop in bio / email / QR? Prefer hosted SaaS (LinkyCal, Fillout, Tally, Typeform, Jotform, forms.app, Zoho, Google, Microsoft). Need the form only inside WordPress? WPForms. Need it only inside HubSpot pages? HubSpot Forms.
2. **What happens after submit?** If the answer is “a Sheet is enough,” Google Forms or Tally → Sheets is fine. If the answer is “a person we follow up with,” prioritize HubSpot, LinkyCal [contacts](/features/contacts), or a builder plus a CRM you already trust.
3. **What is your monthly volume?** Match free caps honestly. Tally and forms.app (unlimited responses) and Fillout (1,000 / month free) and LinkyCal (500 / month free) and Zoho (500 / month free) are different math from Typeform and Jotform free tiers.
4. **How much design control?** Typeform and Fillout lead on polish. Tally is clean and doc-like. LinkyCal covers hosted + embed + optional custom CSS. Google and Microsoft optimize for familiarity over brand.
5. **Compliance?** Jotform Gold calls out HIPAA features. Enterprise stories vary by vendor. Verify BAAs and data residency on the vendor’s security pages before you collect health or other regulated data.

If your flow ends with “book a call,” keep the form tool focused on capture and hand off to a scheduler. Do not force every form vendor to also be your calendar product. LinkyCal happens to include scheduling in the broader product, but for this keyword the form should win on form jobs first.

### Decision paths by situation

**You are launching a waitlist this week.** Use Tally or forms.app if you expect a spike and refuse to think about response caps. Use Fillout if you also want payments or richer logic on day one. Use LinkyCal if the waitlist should become a contact list you nurture, not a CSV you download once.

**You sell services and every lead should become a conversation.** Prefer HubSpot Forms (if you live in HubSpot) or LinkyCal (hosted link + contacts + workflows). Typeform works when the brand experience is part of the sales motion and volume fits the plan.

**You run a WordPress brochure site for local clients.** WPForms keeps editing inside WordPress. If the same agency manages ten Webflow sites and three WordPress sites, a hosted SaaS form (Fillout, Tally, LinkyCal) reduces plugin drift.

**You are in a school or nonprofit on Google Workspace.** Google Forms is enough for internal polls. Move to Fillout, Tally, or forms.app when the form faces donors or parents and needs better branding or file handling.

**You already pay for Zoho CRM.** Zoho Forms reduces integration work. Do not add Zoho Forms just because it is cheap if the rest of your stack is HubSpot or Salesforce—you will recreate the sync problem you were trying to avoid.

**You need fillable-style intake that feels like paperwork.** Jotform’s templates and PDF-related workflows are hard to beat. Pair with clear submission quotas so a viral campaign does not freeze collection on the free tier.

### Shareable form vs embedded form vs WordPress shortcode

Searchers often mix three distribution models:

- **Hosted share link.** The form lives on the vendor’s domain (or custom domain on paid plans). Best for email, social, QR, and “link in bio.” This is the core job for most “best form builder” searches.
- **Embed.** The same form appears inside your page. Best when the surrounding content sells the ask (pricing page, webinar landing page).
- **CMS-native plugin.** Fields and entries live in WordPress (or similar). Best when editors refuse to leave `wp-admin`.

LinkyCal supports hosted links and embeds, with optional action URL / API / custom CSS when you need tighter integration. WPForms is plugin-first. HubSpot is CRM-page-first. Knowing which distribution model you need eliminates half the list immediately.

## Common form builder use cases (and what we would pick)

### Lead capture for a marketing site

Goal: email + context, spam filtered, contact stored, notify sales.

- **HubSpot already in place:** HubSpot Forms.
- **No full CRM suite yet:** LinkyCal ([forms](/features/forms) → [contacts](/features/contacts) → [workflows](/features/workflows)).
- **High volume, low budget:** Fillout or Tally, then sync to your CRM.

Keep the form short. Add conditional fields only when the answer changes routing (enterprise vs self-serve, product interest, region).

### Client intake and applications

Goal: multi-step form, file upload, branching, internal notification, durable record.

Fillout, Jotform, Zoho Forms, and LinkyCal all handle multi-step intake well. Choose Zoho if approvals are mandatory inside Zoho. Choose Jotform if you need a template that already looks like your paperwork. Choose LinkyCal if the applicant should become a contact you keep working after accept/reject.

### Event registration and RSVPs

Google Forms or Microsoft Forms are fine for internal events. For external events with branding and payment, use Fillout, Tally, forms.app, or Jotform. Export the guest list early and test the confirmation email path before you open registration.

### Surveys and research

Typeform still wins when survey feel matters. Google Forms wins when the team will analyze in Sheets and nobody cares about brand. Fillout and forms.app cover the middle: better UX than Google, less expensive at volume than Typeform for many teams.

### Payment-enabled forms

Confirm gateway support and whether payment submissions burn a separate quota. Fillout documents Stripe-style collection on free. Jotform publishes distinct payment submission limits by plan. WPForms routes payments through Stripe, PayPal, Square, and others on paid licenses. Never assume “form submissions” and “payment submissions” share one counter.

### Internal request forms (IT, HR, facilities)

Microsoft Forms or Google Forms usually beat specialized SaaS on politics alone—people already have accounts. Upgrade to Zoho Forms or Jotform when you need approvals, audit trails, or PDF packets.

## Evaluation Criteria & Testing Methodology

We scored each tool the same way a practitioner would during a two-hour trial, not a feature bingo card.

### Criteria we weighted

**1. Time to first shareable link.** Minutes from signup to a URL you can open in an incognito window. Deducted points for forced website installs when the job was “send a link.”

**2. Form building quality.** Field types, multi-step / pages, conditional logic, validation, mobile layout, and whether the editor fights you.

**3. Distribution.** Hosted link, embed options, and (where relevant) WordPress or CRM-native placement. Extra credit for plain HTML action URLs when teams need to keep their own markup.

**4. Free-tier honesty.** Published submission / form / storage caps from vendor pricing pages. We prefer clear ceilings over “unlimited*” with silent fair-use traps, but we note fair-use language when vendors disclose it (Tally).

**5. After-submit operations.** Email notifications, spam controls, exports, integrations, and whether contacts become durable records. This is where LinkyCal’s [workflows](/features/workflows) and contacts layer were evaluated as product surface, not as a bolt-on.

**6. Pricing clarity.** Public monthly and yearly figures when shown. We did not invent numbers. Unclear prices are labeled “check current pricing.”

**7. Fit to “form builder” intent.** Tools that only solve headless endpoints without a visual shareable form were out of scope for the main list (covered in [best form backend services](/blog/best-form-backend-services)).

### How we tested

For each tool we created a realistic lead / intake form: contact fields, a conditional branch (e.g. budget), an optional file upload when the plan allowed it, and a thank-you state. We published a shareable link, submitted test entries from a second browser, and checked whether notifications and response views behaved as documented. We read current public pricing and feature pages rather than relying on third-party roundups alone. AI form generation was noted where vendors advertise it (Fillout, Typeform, Jotform, WPForms AI, Zoho prompt-to-form), but AI was not a ranking trump card over shareable reliability.

### What we did not do

We did not run multi-week conversion experiments across production traffic. Completion-rate claims in vendor marketing were treated as marketing. We did not negotiate enterprise contracts. Security and compliance claims were checked at the feature-label level (e.g. “HIPAA available on Gold”) and should be validated with legal / security review before regulated use.

### Scoring snapshot (qualitative)

| --- | --- |
| Fastest shareable link | Tally, Google Forms, forms.app, LinkyCal, Fillout |
| Richest free usage | Tally, forms.app, Fillout, Google Forms |
| Brand / UX polish | Typeform, Fillout |
| Template / ops breadth | Jotform, WPForms, Zoho Forms |

No tool won every row. That is expected. “Best form builder” in 2026 means best for a defined job.

### Practical checklist we used on every tool

1. Sign up without a sales call.
2. Create a form with at least eight fields and one conditional branch.
3. Publish and open the share link on mobile.
4. Submit twice; confirm both responses appear.
5. Trigger or configure an email notification.
6. Export or view raw response data.
7. Read the pricing page for form caps, response caps, storage, and seats.
8. Note whether spam protection is default or a paid toggle.
9. Note whether contacts become first-class records or only rows in a responses table.
10. Note embed and (if any) HTML action / API paths for later migration.

That checklist is intentionally boring. Boring is how you avoid buying a demo-ware builder that collapses on the second real campaign.

### Notes on AI features in 2026

AI form generation showed up across the SERP for “best form builder,” including AI-focused listicles. In our trials, AI was useful for:

- Turning a rough brief into a first field list.
- Importing questions from a PDF or existing form.
- Suggesting wording or follow-up questions.

AI was not useful as a substitute for:

- Knowing your monthly volume.
- Deciding where contacts should live.
- Configuring spam controls and notification routing.
- Checking whether free-tier branding is acceptable on a customer-facing page.

If a vendor’s homepage leads with AI and buries submission limits, read the pricing page before the feature tour.

## Pricing and free-tier patterns worth watching

Across this set, free plans usually throttle one of four levers:

1. **Responses / submissions per month** (Typeform, Jotform, LinkyCal, Zoho, Fillout).
2. **Number of forms** (Jotform, forms.app free, Zoho free).
3. **Storage / file size** (Jotform, forms.app, Zoho).
4. **Branding / custom domain / CSS** (almost everyone).

Unlimited-response free plans (Tally, forms.app responses, Google Forms) shift the constraint to fair use, form count, storage, or design controls. Paid plans then sell branding removal, seats, analytics, compliance, and integration depth.

Hidden costs to verify on the vendor’s own billing page before you launch:

- Payment submission counters separate from form submissions.
- Seat limits when a second teammate needs edit access.
- File upload caps on application forms.
- Webhook or API access gated to higher tiers (varies widely).
- Removability of vendor badge on customer-facing pages.

We cite only figures we could confirm on public pricing or product pages. When Zoho’s dollar amounts were clearer in a plan picker than in static HTML, we left dollars as “check current pricing” rather than guess.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best overall online form builder in 2026?

There is no single winner for every team. For a **shareable hosted form** plus contacts and follow-up workflows without duct-tape, [LinkyCal](/features/forms) is the strongest fit in this review. For unlimited free submissions, choose **Tally**. For conversational brand UX, choose **Typeform**. For template breadth and specialty ops forms, choose **Jotform**. For WordPress-only sites, choose **WPForms**. For HubSpot CRM shops, use **HubSpot Forms**. Pick from the comparison table using your volume, stack, and after-submit needs.

### Which form builder offers the best free plan with generous response limits?

**Tally** offers unlimited forms and submissions on free (fair use). **forms.app** offers unlimited responses with a 5-form cap on free. **Fillout** offers 1,000 responses / month with unlimited forms and seats. **LinkyCal** offers **500 submissions / month** on free with contacts and workflows included in the product story. **Zoho Forms** also lists 500 submissions / month on free with a 3-form cap. **Google Forms** remains fully free for Workspace-style collection. **Typeform** and **Jotform** free tiers are tighter on responses (Jotform: 100 / month; Typeform: limited free responses — verify live).

### How do AI form builders compare to traditional visual drag-and-drop builders?

AI helps you draft structure faster: prompt-to-form, PDF import, or “smart edit” inside an existing builder. It does not replace the evaluation criteria above. You still need a shareable link, clear limits, spam controls, and a plan for contacts. Fillout, Typeform, Jotform, WPForms, and Zoho all advertise AI-assisted creation. Treat AI as a setup accelerator. Judge the product on the published form and the post-submit path.

### What is the best form builder plugin or solution for WordPress sites?

**WPForms** is the dedicated WordPress answer: build and manage entries inside WordPress, with a free Lite tier and paid licenses for payments, marketing integrations, and advanced fields. If your site is WordPress but you prefer a hosted SaaS link (for QR codes, email, or multi-site reuse), Fillout, Tally, LinkyCal, Typeform, or Jotform can still be embedded on WordPress pages. Choose WPForms when WordPress admin is the system of record; choose hosted SaaS when the link is the system of record.

### How do I choose the right form creator software for my business needs?

Write down: (1) monthly expected submissions, (2) whether you need a hosted link or CMS-native placement, (3) where contacts should live, (4) required integrations, (5) compliance constraints. Then eliminate tools that fail a hard constraint (e.g. WordPress-only, HIPAA, HubSpot-only). Trial two finalists with the same real form. The right choice is the one your team actually publishes and follows up on—not the one with the longest feature list.

### Which is the best form builder for lead generation?

For lead gen, optimize for completion and CRM hygiene. HubSpot Forms if HubSpot is the CRM. LinkyCal if you want a shareable form plus [contacts](/features/contacts) and [workflows](/features/workflows) without living inside a full marketing suite. Typeform if the branded experience lifts conversion enough to justify response pricing. Fillout or Tally if you need volume on a budget and will sync to a CRM via native integration or Zapier.

### Do I need a form builder or a form backend?

If non-developers will edit fields and you need a link to share, you need a **form builder** (this article). If engineers own the UI and you only need an endpoint, spam filtering, and webhooks, read [best form backend services](/blog/best-form-backend-services). Some products (including LinkyCal) offer both a visual builder and deeper API / action URL options—use the builder path first unless you already have a custom frontend.

### Can I accept payments in an online form?

Yes, on many builders. Fillout supports payments on its free plan. Jotform, Typeform, Tally, forms.app, Zoho Forms, and WPForms (paid) all support payment fields or gateway integrations. Always confirm transaction fees, payout country support, and whether payment submissions count against separate quotas (Jotform lists distinct payment submission limits).

### Which is the best program to create a fillable form?

For a **shareable online fillable form**, any hosted builder in this list works—Fillout, Jotform, Typeform, Tally, LinkyCal, forms.app, Zoho, Google Forms, or Microsoft Forms. For **WordPress page fillable forms**, use WPForms. For **PDF-like workflows and document packs**, Jotform is usually the shortest path. “Fillable form” in search often means “online form people can complete,” not a desktop PDF editor.

### Which AI form builder is the best right now?

There is no durable AI-only winner. Fillout’s prompt / PDF import flow is strong for speed. Typeform leans into AI-assisted creation and clarification features on paid plans. Jotform markets AI Agents alongside classic forms. WPForms AI helps WordPress users draft forms in-admin. Zoho offers prompt-to-form generation. Pick the AI assistant attached to the builder you already want for limits, hosting, and CRM fit—not the other way around.

### Is Google Forms still good enough in 2026?

Yes, for internal surveys, classroom use, and lightweight RSVPs tied to Sheets. No, when you need brand control, serious spam defense for public lead gen, multi-step sales intake, or a contact record that drives follow-ups. Graduate when the form is part of revenue or customer onboarding.

### How is a form builder different from Mailchimp or other email tools?

Email platforms sometimes include signup forms, but they optimize for lists and campaigns. Dedicated form builders optimize for field logic, file uploads, branching intake, embeds, and response management. If your only field is email, an ESP signup form may be enough. If you collect project details, budgets, uploads, or quiz scores, use a real form builder and sync contacts afterward.

### What should I migrate away from first?

Migrate when you hit a hard ceiling (responses, branding, missing logic) or when post-submit work is manual every time. Export CSV responses, rebuild the highest-traffic form first, run both links in parallel for a week, then retire the old link. Prefer builders that support embeds and redirects so you can swap the underlying form without changing every landing page URL at once.
